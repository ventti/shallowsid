Privacy

ShallowSID · last updated 2026-09-24

In short

ShallowSID works without accounts and without tracking. Unless you turn on Sync, nothing you do leaves your browser. If you turn it on, an encrypted copy of your playlists and settings is stored so your devices can share it. Nobody but your devices can read it.

Stored in your browser

  • Playlists, favorites, recently played tunes and playlists, play counts, sound presets and preferences, in this browser's local storage (keys starting with shallowsid.).
  • A copy of the app's own files and libraries, in the browser's cache, so it opens offline. Nothing about you is in it.
  • This is needed for features you use, stays on your device, and is never sent anywhere unless you turn on Sync. Clearing the site's data removes it.

Sync (optional, off by default)

  • What is stored: one encrypted record with your playlists, favorites, recently played tunes and playlists, play counts, sound presets, two preferences (selected sound preset, pre-rendering) and the list of devices using the key: for each, its OS, browser, whether it runs as an installed app, when it last synced, its public key for handing over a new sync key, and a place guessed from its time zone (such as “Helsinki”; no location services are used). It is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM, using a key derived from your sync key. The record's ID is also derived from the sync key and reveals nothing about you. Only devices with the sync key can change or delete the record: knowing its ID is not enough.
  • Removing a device: the data moves to a new key. The old record is replaced by a small one holding the new key, encrypted separately for each device you kept, with a key only that device has. It is kept so devices that were offline can still follow, and can't be changed or deleted.
  • What is not stored: no name, email, account or device identifier. The sync key itself never leaves your devices.
  • QR code: the sync key can be shown as a QR code (a link with the key after #, which browsers never send to a server). Scanning one in the app uses your camera only while the scanner is open; images are read on your device and never stored or sent.
  • Where: Google Cloud Firestore (Firebase), in the EU region europe-north1 (Finland), under Google's data processing terms. Like any web service, Google processes technical data such as IP addresses to deliver requests; ShallowSID does not collect or log them.
  • Why: to provide the sync you asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • How long: until you delete it with Sync → Delete Synced Data. It is not deleted automatically. Records written before September 2026 were moved to a new format the next time a device synced; the old copies can't be changed from the app and are removed by the maintainer.
  • Your rights: Sync → Delete Synced Data removes the record immediately. Because the data is encrypted with a key only you hold, we cannot look it up, read it or recover it for you. Without the key it can't be linked to you.

Live links to playlists (optional)

  • What is stored: when you choose Share → Live Link, that playlist's name and the list of tunes are stored unencrypted, so that anyone with the link can open it. Nothing else is stored with it: no name, account or device identifier. A random proof value lets only your devices change it.
  • Who can see it: anyone who has the link. Links are not listed or searchable anywhere, but anyone you send one to can pass it on.
  • Where: Google Cloud Firestore, europe-north1 (Finland), as above.
  • How long: until you choose Stop Sharing. That removes the name and tunes at once; the link then only shows “no longer shared”. It is not deleted automatically.
  • Don't put personal information in the name of a playlist you share.

Tune tags and curators (invite only)

  • Reading tags: everyone sees tags such as “Ballad” on tunes. They come with the site itself, so playing and browsing don't contact the database.
  • What is stored for a curator: people invited as curators pick tags from a fixed list. Each tagged tune stores its tags, when they were last changed and a random curator ID. The curator record holds that ID, its role, whether it is active, a proof value that lets only the curator's devices use it, and which invite it came from. No name, email, account or device identifier.
  • Who can see who tagged what: the app shows it only to that curator and to admins. The curator ID is random, but anyone reading the database directly can see which tags share one. The name an admin gives an invite stays in the admin's own synced data, encrypted, and is never stored in the database.
  • Where: Google Cloud Firestore, europe-north1 (Finland), as above.
  • How long: tags stay until a curator changes them. A curator record stays until the maintainer removes it; an admin turning a curator off only marks it as off.
  • Why: to provide the tags (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) and, for curators, the role they accepted (Art. 6(1)(b)).

Bot protection (only with Sync, live links or curating)

  • So that only this site can use its database, requests for Sync, live links and curating carry a Firebase App Check token. To get one, your browser runs Google reCAPTCHA in the background; there is no puzzle to solve. It loads only when you use Sync or open or share a live link, not when you just play tunes.
  • reCAPTCHA sends Google technical data about your browser and how the page is used, which Google uses to tell people from bots. The Google Privacy Policy and Terms of Service apply.
  • Why: to keep the service you asked for safe from abuse (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)).

Other services

  • Tunes are downloaded from the High Voltage SID Collection (www.hvsc.c64.org) when you play them.
  • The app itself is served by GitHub Pages; libraries and fonts load from jsDelivr and Google Fonts. These services see your IP address when your browser fetches files from them.
