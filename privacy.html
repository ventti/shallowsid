Privacy

In short

Your playlists, listening history and settings stay in your browser by default.

If you enable Sync, ShallowSID stores an encrypted copy of this information in the cloud for use across your devices. Live links publish your chosen playlist as readable data.

Your browser also contacts services that deliver the app, fonts, libraries and tunes. These services receive technical data, including your IP address. Sync, live links and curator features also use Google's bot protection.

Stored in your browser

  • ShallowSID stores playlists, favorites, recently played tunes and playlists, play counts, sound presets and preferences in local storage. The storage keys start with shallowsid..
  • Your browser caches app files and libraries so the app can open offline.
  • This storage supports the features you use. Clear the site's data in your browser to remove it. Server copies stay until you delete them through Sync or stop sharing each live link.

Sync (optional, off by default)

  • What is stored: an encrypted record contains your playlists, favorites, recently played tunes and playlists, play counts and sound presets. It also contains your selected sound preset, pre-rendering preference and a list of devices that use the sync key.
  • Device details: each device has a random ID and a public key for secure key transfers. Its details include its operating system, browser, installed-app status and last sync time. A place name, such as “Helsinki”, comes from its time zone alone.
  • Encryption: your device encrypts the record with AES-256-GCM and a key derived from your sync key. Your devices hold the key needed to read the contents. The record's ID also comes from the sync key. Only devices with the sync key can change or delete the active record.
  • Removing a device: your remaining devices use a new sync key. The old record becomes an encrypted key-transfer record so those devices can reconnect later. Each remaining device can read only its own copy of the new key. Access to the new key stays with the remaining devices.
  • Old key-transfer records: these records remain on the server. The app leaves them unchanged.
  • Personal details: Sync uses a random ID instead of your name or email address. Random IDs and encryption protect your data. Data that can be linked to a person remains personal data. The server stores a new sync key only in encrypted form when devices need a key transfer.
  • QR code: a QR code can contain a link with your sync key after #. This part of the link stays in your browser. Anyone with the key can read your synced data.
  • Camera: the scanner uses your camera while it is open. It reads images on your device, then discards them.
  • Where: Google Cloud Firestore (Firebase) stores the records in europe-north1, Finland. Google also processes technical data, including IP addresses, to deliver and protect requests. The international processing section below also applies.
  • Why: to provide Sync at your request. The legal basis is performance of a contract under GDPR Article 6(1)(b).
  • How long: the active record stays until you use Sync → Delete Synced Data. Inactive records stay until you delete them.
  • After deletion: old key-transfer records remain. They contain encrypted key-transfer data rather than the old playlists or settings.
  • Older copies: records from before September 2026 move to the new format when a device next syncs. The maintainer removes those old copies manually. Until then, they remain on the server, with no scheduled removal date.
  • Deletion: Delete Synced Data deletes the active server record when the operation succeeds. It turns Sync off and removes the sync key from this device. Data on your devices stays. Old key-transfer records, older copies and live links remain. Use Stop Sharing to remove a live link's playlist content.

Live links to playlists (optional)

  • What is stored: Share → Live Link publishes the playlist's name and tune list as readable data. Google Cloud Firestore stores this data in Finland, as described above. A random proof value controls changes to the playlist.
  • Who can read it: anyone with the link. People find the playlist through its link. They can forward the link or keep copies. Use playlist names that protect your privacy.
  • Why: to publish, update and open the playlist at your request. The legal basis is performance of a contract under GDPR Article 6(1)(b).
  • How long: the playlist stays until you choose Stop Sharing.
  • After Stop Sharing: the name and tunes are removed. A record remains so the link can show “no longer shared”. Copies held by other people remain under their control.

Tune tags and curators (invite only)

  • Reading tags: tags, such as “Ballad”, come with the site files. Your browser reads them from those files.
  • Curator data: invited curators choose tags from a fixed list. Each tagged tune stores the tags, the time of the last change and a random curator ID. A curator record stores that ID, role, active status, a proof value and the source invite. The proof value controls access. Curators use a random ID instead of a name or email address.
  • Who can read it: the app shows each curator's changes to that curator and to admins. Direct database access can reveal which tags share a curator ID. This can link changes to the same curator.
  • Invite names: an admin's name for an invite stays in the admin's encrypted synced data.
  • Where: Google Cloud Firestore stores these records in Finland, as described above.
  • How long: tags stay until a curator changes them. Curator records stay until the maintainer removes them. Deactivated curator records also stay until the maintainer removes them.
  • Why: our legitimate interest is to maintain useful tune tags and record their source, under GDPR Article 6(1)(f). For the curator role you accept, the legal basis is performance of a contract under Article 6(1)(b).

Bot protection

  • Sync, live links and curator features use Firebase App Check with Google reCAPTCHA Enterprise. This helps reject automated abuse of the database.
  • reCAPTCHA starts with the first database request. It can continue to refresh its security token while the app remains open. These database features trigger the security check.
  • Google processes technical data about your device, browser and use of the page to detect bots. reCAPTCHA also uses the _grecaptcha cookie for this security check.
  • Why: our legitimate interest is to prevent abuse and keep the service available, under GDPR Article 6(1)(f).
  • Google processes reCAPTCHA customer data on our behalf under its Cloud Data Processing Addendum and service terms.

Other services and international processing

  • The High Voltage SID Collection supplies tunes when you play them. GitHub Pages delivers the app. jsDelivr and Google Fonts deliver libraries and fonts. These services receive your IP address and request details, such as the requested file, to deliver their content.
  • Our legitimate interest is to deliver the app and music you request, under GDPR Article 6(1)(f). Providers also process technical data under their own terms, including for security and service operation.
  • Firestore records are stored in Finland. Providers can process technical data elsewhere, including outside the European Economic Area and in the United States.
  • Google's data-processing terms provide transfer safeguards, including the European Commission's standard contractual clauses where applicable. These clauses set duties for protecting transferred personal data. See Google's data-processing terms for the safeguards and how to obtain copies.
  • For other providers' processing, retention and transfer safeguards, see the GitHub privacy statement, jsDelivr privacy policy and Google Fonts privacy information.

Your rights

You control your encrypted Sync contents through the app. Use the app to view and change your data. Use Sync → Delete Synced Data to delete the active server copy.

Only your devices hold the key needed to read your Sync contents. We cannot read, correct or provide a readable copy of those contents.

For personal data we can identify as yours, you can request access, correction, deletion, restriction of processing and a portable copy. These rights apply subject to the conditions in the GDPR.

You can object to processing based on legitimate interests.

Contact us at shallowsid@extend.fi to make a request. We normally respond within one month.

If the GDPR permits more time, we will explain the reason within that month.

We may need information that connects you to the relevant record before we can act. Where we cannot identify your data, GDPR Article 11 can limit our duty to act on these requests.

Your devices control access to your encrypted online contents. Keep your sync key private.

If you lose the sync key, the key and the online data encrypted with it are unrecoverable.

You can complain to a data protection authority, including the Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman in Finland. You can also complain to the authority where you live or work in the European Economic Area.

Choose Sync, live links or curator features when you want them. Each feature uses the data and security checks described above. Tune playback is available independently.
